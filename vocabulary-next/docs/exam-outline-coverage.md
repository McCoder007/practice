# Exam coverage: old banks vs. Study by Exam Topic

Checked 2026-10-23 against the NIC National Nail Technology Theory exam outline (Candidate Information Bulletin, effective Sept 1 2025, revised March 1 2026). Every question in Practice Bank A (156), B (28), C (164), Kelly Nail School (89) and Study by Exam Topic (460) was sorted into the outline's 50 named items. Per-question results: [exam-outline-coverage.csv](exam-outline-coverage.csv).

## Bottom line

- **Before (A, B, C, Kelly):** 437 questions, but only about 400 different ones. About 1 in 8 were not on the exam at all (mostly Bank C: business, life skills, general science). The rest leaned hard on infection control and anatomy and skipped most of the hands-on service steps. **7 outline items were never asked** (work station setup, cuticle care, cleaning the nails, removing massage lotion, polishing, paraffin, hot towels/stones) and 8 more were asked only once or twice (including powder dip, aftercare, equipment and buffing). Manicure and pedicure is 18% of the exam but was 8% of the old questions.
- **Now (Study by Exam Topic):** the mix of questions matches the exam weights closely, and every outline item has at least one question. That's a big step up. It is not yet "nothing missed": a few outline items are still thin (buffing, removing massage product, hot towels/stones, powder dip steps and removal, tip removal), and some facts the old banks taught don't appear in the new sets.
- **Old facts vs. new sets:** of the 379 old questions that are on the exam, the new sets teach the same fact for 174 (46%). Another 139 are close (the topic is taught, but not that exact fact or name), and 66 are not taught at all. After throwing out trivia and doubtful keys, **126 old questions test exam-worthy facts the new sets don't fully teach.** Many repeat each other, so it's fewer distinct facts. They're listed at the end, by outline item.

**The 126 exam-worthy gaps, after this update:**
- 49 are now tested by a new question, and 27 more are now in a new question's Why or Lock line.
- About 18 were already explained in a main set's Why line (e.g. epidermis is outermost, acquired immunity, allergies at consultation, nail water content, solutions don't separate). They're taught, just not asked as their own question.
- About 14 **can't be added** because Milady doesn't say them, or says something different: bacilli/rod shape, onychauxis, "older nails are brittle" (Milady says only "grow more slowly"), tibia = "shin bone", calcaneus = "heel bone", ringworm = tinea, routes of entry, hot oil manicure, buffing direction, pausing during a heat spike, "gloves first" when a client bleeds (Milady: stop the service first), nitrocellulose (Milady says "cellulose-type film formers"), the "toxic trio".

These are low-yield. They could be a second small round (about 10 questions) if wanted.

## What to add so nothing is missed

*(Written before the update above. Most of this list is now in sets 1D–7F; see the update for what was left out and why.)*

Each of these was checked by searching the 460 topic questions. Each fact is either absent or appears only as a wrong-answer choice.

**Outline items that are still thin**
- 6.5 Buffing: how to buff (one direction, light pressure), why over-buffing thins the nail and makes heat, and chamois buffer with paste for shine.
- 6.8 Removing massage product: *how* (wipe the plate with remover or cleanser on a lint-free pad). Only the *why* is taught (6B-10).
- 6.13 Thermal add-ons: hot towels from a towel warmer (test the heat first), heated mitts and booties, and hot stones heated only in a stone warmer. Only "stones feel comfortably warm" is asked.
- 6.2 Soak: the purpose of a finger soak (it softens the cuticle) is never asked. Only the foot soak is. 6.4 Cleanse nails has just 2 questions (scrubbing the plate with a nail brush).
- 7.4 Powder dip: the activator's job, the step order, and dip removal. The current 7D questions cover only what dip is, hygiene and changing the color.
- 7.1 Tips: tip types (full-well, partial-well, well-less), the tip angle at application, and **tip and wrap removal** (no removal question exists for tips or wraps).
- 7.2 Acrylics: keep a hairline gap from the skin, the three zones, and wet vs. dry bead ratios. Only the medium bead is asked; the wet ratio shows up only as a wrong choice. Also EMA as the normal monomer (MMA being banned is taught, EMA is not).
- 5.5 E-file: "ring of fire" (keep the bit flat, reduce pressure), and non-acetone remover on tips and acrylic.

**Facts asked before but not taught now (the most important ones)**
- Bacteria shapes (cocci, bacilli, spirilla); local vs. general infection (a boil is local); natural vs. acquired immunity; hepatitis and HIV basics beyond "bloodborne pathogen".
- Cleaning is the lowest level of infection control; mix and use disinfectant per the manufacturer; household bleach = sodium hypochlorite; quats as the common disinfectant.
- Use a spatula to take cream from a jar; a fresh towel for each client; a dropped tool must be replaced or disinfected; emery boards can't be disinfected; blood-exposure steps (gloves, double-bag); the term "exposure incident".
- Get the SDS from the manufacturer; the SDS lists hazardous ingredients; wear a dust mask when e-filing; routes of overexposure (breathing in, through skin, swallowing); the word "ergonomics"; repetitive-motion injuries such as carpal tunnel.
- Skin: the epidermis is the outer layer; skin functions (protection, excretion); melanin and melanocytes; dermis layers; primary lesions (vesicle, pustule, bulla, wheal) and ulcer; vitiligo; melanoma and the rest of ABCDE (only "A" is taught).
- Nails: definitions of nail bed, nail body = plate, and free edge; what the cuticle does (seals out germs); older clients' nails (brittle, slow growing); onychauxis; eggshell nails; tinea as the name for ringworm; signs of nail fungus and athlete's foot.
- Bones, muscles and nerves: tibia = "shin bone", patella = kneecap, calcaneus = heel bone, metatarsals, fibula, flexors, supinators, adductors, digital nerves, radial and ulnar arteries.
- Chemistry: nitrocellulose (film former in polish), what each polish ingredient does, solvent/solute (taught only lightly), surfactants, the word "polymerization", oligomer as the main gel ingredient, and the "toxic trio" (formaldehyde, toluene, DBP).
- Tools: pumice is an abrasive (an official NIC sample question), grit ranges, sorting items into equipment, implements, supplies and products, and nippers for loose dead skin only.
- Services: hot oil manicure, joint movements in massage, never massaging over cuts, bruises, rashes or varicose veins, thin coats of polish, "fill" defined, check the stress area for strength, keep the hand flat with gel polish, and if a gel heat spike happens, pause and then finish the full cure time.

## Answer keys to look at

- **Kelly-057** "Ingrown hair is commonly associated with: Bacteria". Probably wrong: an ingrown hair is hair curling back into the skin. It's also not a nail-tech fact.
- **Kelly-041** says to put on gloves *first* when a client bleeds. Bank A and the new sets say stop the service first. These conflict.
- **Bank A q001/q003/q005** (e-file at 90° and 30°) conflict with the old "hold the bit flat/parallel" answers. Worth checking against Milady.
- **Kelly-046 / Bank B 028** say to follow the manufacturer's contact time for pedicure spa disinfectant. New 8A-09 says a fixed 10 minutes. Not wrong, but it could confuse the learner.
- Topic set **7B-01** (medium bead = 1.5× liquid to powder) was flagged during the check but is correct: it matches the cited Milady page.

## How this was done, and limits

- Each question got one main outline item (sometimes a second). Edge cases were decided by fixed rules; for example, "is it safe to do the service?" counts as pre-service, not anatomy. Counts could move by a few if someone else sorted them.
- "Covered" means a topic question tests the same fact, so a learner who only studied the topic sets would get the old question right. "Partial" usually means the idea is taught but not that exact name or detail (e.g. "tibia" is taught, "shin bone" is not).
- The NIC exam also draws on Pivot Point and Tammy Taylor, not only Milady. No question bank can promise every fact; this check measures against the outline and Milady.
- Not included: Official Practice (100) and Milady Exam Review, which weren't part of this question.
